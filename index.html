💡 Core Academic Insights & Findings

  • Interactive Process Responsiveness: In Variable Burst mode, processes with short burst times ($\le 3$ units) finish entirely in $Q_0$ without suffering queue degradation. This keeps their turnaround time exceptionally low!
  • CPU-Bound Degradation: Processes with long execution requirements exhaust their 3-unit time slice and are demoted to $Q_1$, and subsequently to $Q_2$. This prevents heavy computations from starving short or I/O-bound processes.
  • Fairness Under Fixed Workloads: When burst times are uniform, Round Robin time slicing distributes waiting time symmetrically across all processes in arrival order.

⚙️ Scheduling Rules Modeled

  • Multi-Level Priority Queues: 3 queues ($Q_0$: High, $Q_1$: Medium, $Q_2$: Low).
  • Round Robin Slicing: Strict time quantum of 3 units ($Q = 3$).
  • Priority Feedback Demotion: A process that uses up its 3 units without completing is demoted to the next lower queue ($Q_i o Q_{i+1}$).
  • Priority Preemption: If a new process arrives at higher priority ($Q_0$) while a lower queue process is running, the CPU immediately dispatches the higher priority process.
  • Fixed Simulation Horizon ($T_{sim}$): Evaluates completed processes within a designated observation timeframe and computes interim metrics for in-flight tasks.
