🧮 Process Scheduling Mathematics & Step-by-Step Derivations
Turnaround Time (TAT)
TAT = Completion Time - Arrival Time
Total elapsed interval spent by the process from arrival until full termination.
Waiting Time (WT)
WT = Turnaround Time - Burst Time
Time spent waiting in ready queues without CPU allocation.
Response Time (RT)
RT = First CPU Dispatch - Arrival Time
Interval until the user receives the initial reaction from the operating system.
Average Waiting Time (AWT)
AWT = (Σ WT) / N_completed
Primary benchmark indicating queuing delay across completed workloads.

📘 Problem Statement #21 & Operating Systems Theory

"UNIX operating system uses Round robin Time slicing with multilevel feedback for process scheduling. Assume that the processes (PCB's) arrive at random. Assume the time slice is 3 units. Simulate Round robin time slicing with fixed and variable (Randomized) burst times. Run the processes for fixed amount of time and find the average waiting time, average turn-around time of the processes completed."

Key Mechanisms Verified:

  • Priority Feedback Decay: Long jobs forfeit priority upon quantum ($Q=3$) exhaustion.
  • Dynamic Preemption: Newly arriving interactive processes preempt lower-priority batch processes.
  • Fairness & Responsiveness: Preserves interactive low-latency for short tasks while guaranteeing continuous throughput for heavy computations.
